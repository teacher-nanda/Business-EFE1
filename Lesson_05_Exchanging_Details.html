🔑 Key Language

Key Language: Exchanging Contact Details

It is useful to know how to ask for contact information from a client or co-worker. Certain stock phrases can be adapted to many different situations.

These products look great! Do you have a website?
Yes! Here's my card with all the details.
In UK English, "Have you got...?" is also common.
How can I reach you for more information?
Just give me a call. / Call me. Here's my number. / Drop me a line.
"Give me a ring" is also common, particularly in UK English.

🔑 Key Language

Pronunciation: Email Addresses

There are set conventions for how to pronounce email address symbols such as "@" and "." in English.

sue@super-cleaning_team.com
athyphenunderscoredot
💡 This is pronounced as one word, but domains like .co.jp and .co.uk are pronounced with initials: "dot co dot yoo kay."

🔑 Key Language

Key Language: Short Answers

You will often hear short answers such as "Yes, I am" in English-speaking workplaces. It is more polite to use a short answer than to just answer "Yes" or "No."

Is this your current email address?
Yes, it is. Email me any time.
Are you on JobConnect?
No, I'm not. I have a blog, though.
Does your company have a website?
No, it doesn't. It does have a mailing list.
Do you have a business card?
Yes, I do. All my details are on here.
💡 You can use long answers in more formal conversations, or when you want to add more information in your response.

🔁 Review

What have we learned? 📚

💳 Business Cards

  • name · job title · company name
  • address · phone number · website · email address

🔤 Pronouncing Emails

  • @ = at   - = hyphen   _ = underscore   . = dot
  • .co.uk → "dot co dot yoo kay"

🔗 Asking for & Giving Contact Details

  • Do you have a website? → Yes! Here's my card.
  • How can I reach you? → Just give me a call.
  • Drop me a line. / Give me a ring.
  • Is this your email? → Yes, it is.

✅ Short Answers

  • Do you have...? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does it have...? → Yes, it does. / No, it doesn't.
  • Is this...? → Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
  • Are these...? → Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
"Do you have a business card? Yes, I do — here's my email address. Just drop me a line!"
