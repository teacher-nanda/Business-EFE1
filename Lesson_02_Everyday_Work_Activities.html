📐 Grammar

Key Language — The Present Simple

📌 When to use the Present Simple

Use the present simple to talk about things you do regularly, such as your daily tasks or everyday work routines.
"Every morning, we prepare the food and Justin sets the tables."
✅ Use it for:
  • Daily routines & habits
  • Regular tasks at work
  • Things that are generally true
  • Job descriptions
⏰ Common time expressions:
every day every morning on Mondays usually twice a day at 11am once every two months twice every week
📐 Grammar

How to Form the Present Simple

With regular verbs, use the base form of the verb to make the present simple with "I," "you," "we," and "they."
With "he," "she," and "it," add "s" to the base form.

Subject Verb Rest of sentence
I / You / We / They prepare the food every morning.
He / She prepares
📝 Regular verbs (+s)
work → works
prepare → prepares
read → reads
eat → eats
⚠️ Special: "be" forms
I am (I'm)
He / She is (He's / She's)
We / You / They are (We're)
⚠️ Spelling changes
go → goes
do → does
have → has
finish → finishes
💡 Remember from Lesson 1: We use these forms to introduce ourselves too! "My name's Alisha." → "name's" is a contracted form of "name is".

📐 Grammar

Further Examples — The Present Simple

"Be" with "I" is "I am." The short form is "I'm."
I'm a lifeguard at the local pool.
Present simple form of "be" with "he," "she," and "it."
Mia is an excellent tour guide.
They have a meeting every morning.
We usually stop for tea and coffee at 11.
Stephanie works from home on Mondays.
Present simple form of "be" with "we," "you," and "they."
We are always busy in the evening.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

It's easy to forget to add "s" to the base form of the verb in the present simple with third-person singular pronouns, "he," "she," and "it."

✅ Correct
The CEO works on Sundays.
Add "s" to the base form
❌ Wrong
The CEO work on Sundays.
Missing "s" — this is wrong!
📐 Remember the rule
I / You / We / They
work · eat · travel
He / She / It
works · eats · travels
📌 Special Cases — Collective Nouns
Group words (e.g. staff) → plural
The staff go to lunch at noon.
The staff goes ❌
Department names → singular
The IT Helpdesk is really helpful.
The IT Helpdesk are ❌

OUR TEAM

Meet the manager

Our Head of Customer Services describes a typical workday

Sumiko Akimoto, our Head of Customer Services, describes a typical day at work. "Every morning, even in the winter, I ride my bicycle to work. I arrive at work early and then walk through the departments to talk to the staff. It is important for me to know what is happening in the company so that I can share any useful information with clients. Next, I read my emails and use them to help me write a list of things to do during the day. I rarely do everything on the list, but it's useful to help me plan my day.

During my morning coffee break, I talk to my team members about my list and sometimes delegate tasks to them. At lunchtime, many of my colleagues go to a local Italian restaurant to eat, but I stay in the office and eat a packed lunch. I like to deal with all my emails by 5 o'clock. Sometimes I can leave work at 5:30, but I usually leave at 6 o'clock. To help me relax after work, I turn off my phone as soon as I get home."

🔁 Review

What have we learned? 📚

📐 The Present Simple

  • Use for regular actions & routines
  • I / You / We / They → base form
  • He / She / It → base form + s
  • "be" → am / is / are

⏰ Time Expressions

  • every day / every morning
  • on Mondays / on Thursdays
  • usually / always / sometimes
  • twice a day / at 11am

📝 Key Verbs (work routines)

  • prepare · work · read · travel
  • eat · drink · leave · open
  • bring · walk · shout · stop
  • have · meet · check · write

🔗 Connecting L1 + L2

  • "My name's Alisha. I'm a manager." (L1 + be)
  • "I work at Spandone & Co." (present simple)
  • "Every day, I prepare reports." (L2)
  • Full introduction = who you are + what you do!
"Every morning, we prepare the food and Justin sets the tables. I'm always busy, but I love my job!"
