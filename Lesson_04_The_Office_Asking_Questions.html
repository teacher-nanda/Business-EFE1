🔑 Key Language

Key Language: Simple Questions with "To Be"

It is important to use the correct word order and question words in English, depending on whether the questions are open-ended.

🔑 Key Language

How to Form: Simple Questions with "To Be"

In a statement, the subject comes before the verb. In a question, the subject and verb swap places.

🔑 Key Language

Key Language: Simple Questions with "Do"

To form questions in sentences without the verb "to be," start the question with "do" or "does."

🔑 Key Language

How to Form: Simple Questions with "Do"

"Does" is the third person form of "do." Use the base form of the verb.

🔑 Key Language

Key Language: Asking Open Questions

Use question words such as "when," "where," "how," or "why" to ask questions that can't be answered with "yes" or "no."

Where is the staff room?
Go down to the second floor.
When does Mia start work?
She usually starts at nine.
🔑 Key Language

Further Examples: Open Questions

How does the scanner work?
The auxiliary "do / does" comes before the subject.
Where is the cafeteria?
Invert the subject and the verb to form open questions with "to be."
Who is giving the presentation?
What would you like to drink?
Why is he late?
When does the meeting start?
The main verb comes at the end in questions without "to be."

🔗 Questions with "To Be"

  • This is my desk. → Is this my desk?
  • The rooms are busy. → Are the rooms busy?

🔗 Questions with "Do/Does"

  • Do I / you / we / they have…?
  • Does he / she have…?

❓ Asking Open Questions

  • Where is the staff room?
  • When does the meeting start?
  • How does the scanner work?
  • Why is he late?
"Is this your desk? Do you have a moment? Where is the meeting room, and why is it always busy?"
