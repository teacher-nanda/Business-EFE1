🔑 Key Language

8.1 Key Language: "Have"

Use "have" with nouns to talk about people's qualities or experience.

I have good computer skills.
My assistant has an excellent phone manner.
"Have" is an irregular verb. The third-person form is "has."
🔑 Key Language

8.2 Further Examples: "Have" and "Have Got"

"Have got" is used in informal spoken UK English, and often shortened to 've got / 's got.

He has excellent negotiation skills.
Have you got any catering experience?
They don't have good people skills.
In negatives, "do not" or its short form "don't" sits before "have."
She's got a positive attitude.
This short form of "has got" is informal.
🔑 Key Language

8.3 How to Form Statements Using "Have"

Subject"Have" / "Has"Rest of Sentence
I / You / We / Theyhavegood computer skills.
He / Shehasgood computer skills.

With "he," "she," and "it," use "has."

🔑 Key Language

8.6 Key Language: "A / An / The"

Use "a" or "an" to talk about jobs and workplaces if you are mentioning them for the first time. Use "the" to talk about something specific, or something you have mentioned before.

I'm a waiter. I work in a popular restaurant.
The restaurant is always busy.
"A/an" — first mention of the restaurant. "The" — you've already mentioned it.
🔑 Key Language

8.7 Further Examples: "A / An / The"

I'm an intern at an advertising agency.
Use "an" before a vowel sound.
The agency is next to a café.
Isaac is a good hairdresser.
The hairdresser who works weekends is terrible.

🔑 Key Language

8.10 Key Language: The Zero Article

When English leaves out "a," "an," or "the" before a noun, this is called the zero article. Use the zero article with plurals when you are talking about things in general.

I get very nervous before interviews.
Refers to interviews in general, not specific interviews.
We're looking for people who can sell our products.
Refers to people in general, not specific individuals.
