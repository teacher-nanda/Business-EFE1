Lesson 1 Review: Meeting New Colleagues

📌 Quick recap — Formal: "My name is…", "May I introduce…", "It's a pleasure to meet you." Informal: "I'm…", "This is…", "Nice to meet you."

🎤 Speaking Game — Networking Mixer

Work with a partner. Flip through each card and introduce yourself the way the card asks — then swap roles and try the next one.

👔 Card 1 — Very Formal
It's your first day. You're introduced to Mr. Alvarez, the Regional Director.
Mr. Alvarez"Good morning. I'm Mr. Alvarez, Regional Director. Welcome to the team."
Your turn — introduce yourself formally
"Good morning, Mr. Alvarez. My name is… It's a pleasure to meet you."
😊 Card 2 — Informal
You're grabbing coffee during a break and meet Jamie from the IT team.
Jamie"Hey! I don't think we've met — I'm Jamie."
Your turn — introduce yourself informally
"Hi, I'm… Nice to meet you."
🌐 Card 3 — Introduce a Colleague (Formal)
You're at a client meeting with your colleague, Priya.
Client"Hello — I don't believe we've met before."
Your turn — introduce Priya to the client, formally
"May I introduce my colleague, Priya?"
🤝 Card 4 — Introduce a Friend (Informal)
You bump into your friend Sam in the lobby, together with a new coworker.
Coworker"Oh, hi! I don't think I know your friend."
Your turn — introduce Sam informally
"This is my friend Sam." / "Sam, meet…"

Lesson 2 Review: Everyday Work Activities

📌 Quick recap — Base form with I/you/we/they. Add "-s" with he/she/it: work → works, have → has. Time expressions: every day, every morning, usually, on Mondays.

🕵️ Speaking Game — Guess the Job

One partner reads a Mystery Routine aloud, using the Present Simple. The other partner listens and guesses the job — then flip the card to check.

🔍 Mystery Person 1
She starts work at 6am, every day.
She usually cooks food for many different people.
She doesn't sit down much during her shift.
She often works on Saturdays and Sundays.
She's a chef.
🔍 Mystery Person 2
He works in an office. He has a team meeting every morning.
He usually stops for coffee at 11.
He deals with emails until 5 o'clock.
He's an office manager.
🔍 Mystery Person 3
She doesn't work in an office. She travels to a different client every day.
She usually drives or takes the train.
She has meetings, not emails, most of the time.
She's a sales rep.
🔍 Mystery Person 4
He works from home on Mondays.
He usually starts very early in the morning.
He doesn't see his colleagues much — he mostly emails and calls.
He's a remote consultant.

Lesson 3 Review: Business Around the World

📌 Quick recap — Use "from" + country or a nationality adjective: "from Italy" / "Italian." Contracted negatives: doesn't, don't, isn't, aren't, I'm not.

🌍 Speaking Game — Guess the Origin

Read the clue aloud to your partner without saying the country. Your partner guesses the country and the nationality adjective — then flip the card to check.

🛵 Clue Card 1 — Product
"These stylish scooters aren't made in France or Germany. They're famous worldwide for their design."
From Italy — they're Italian.
⌚ Clue Card 2 — Product
"These watches aren't cheap, and they aren't American. They're famous for precision."
From Switzerland — they're Swiss.
☕ Clue Card 3 — Product
"This coffee isn't from Europe. It's grown in South America and it's very popular."
From Brazil — it's Brazilian.
🧑 Clue Card 4 — Person
"This person isn't British, but works in London."
From Ireland — they're Irish.

Lesson 4 Review: Asking Questions

📌 Quick recap — "To be": Are you…? Is she…? "Do/Does": Do you…? Does he…? Open questions: What/Where/When/Why/How + do/does…?

🔥 Speaking Game — Hot Seat Interview

One partner sits in the Hot Seat and plays the role below. The other partner asks a question of the type shown — then swap and try the next round.

💼 Round 1 — "To Be" Yes/No Question
Hot Seat role: Office Manager. Ask about their job using "to be."
"Is she the CEO?" / "Are you the manager?"
🙋 Round 2 — "Do/Does" Yes/No Question
Hot Seat role: New Colleague. Ask about their routine using "do/does."
"Does he usually arrive late?" / "Do you want tea or coffee?"
🛎️ Round 3 — Open Question (What/Where)
Hot Seat role: Receptionist. Ask an open question with What or Where.
"Where is the stationery cabinet?" / "What time does the meeting start?"
🖥️ Round 4 — Open Question (How/Why)
Hot Seat role: IT Helper. Ask an open question with How or Why.
"How do I scan this document?" / "Why is the cafeteria closed?"

⚡ Quick Warm-Up (optional)

🏢 Speaking Challenge — First Day at a New Office

Work with a partner. Follow the story step by step, chaining together everything from Lessons 1–4: introducing yourself, describing your routine, saying where your company is from, and asking a question.

1️⃣ Step 1 — Reception
You meet Taylor, your new colleague, for the very first time.
Taylor"Hi there! You must be the new hire. I'm Taylor."
Your turn — introduce yourself (Lesson 1)
"Hi, I'm… Nice to meet you." / "My name is… It's a pleasure to meet you."
2️⃣ Step 2 — Desk Tour
Taylor shows you around the office.
Taylor"Great! So, what does a typical day look like for you?"
Your turn — describe your daily routine (Lesson 2)
"I usually start work at… I check my emails every morning… I have a team meeting on Mondays."
3️⃣ Step 3 — Small Talk
Taylor asks about your company.
Taylor"Nice! By the way, where is your company originally from?"
Your turn — say where your company is from (Lesson 3)
"We're from… / Our company is [nationality]. We aren't a small company."
4️⃣ Step 4 — Wrap-Up
Before Taylor heads back to work, you have a chance to ask a question.
Taylor"It was great meeting you! Let me know if you need anything."
Your turn — ask Taylor a question about the office (Lesson 4)
"Is the cafeteria nearby?" / "Do you usually work late?" / "Where is the meeting room?"
